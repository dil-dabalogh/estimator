# Engineering Management - Role & Experience

## 1. Ownership & Impact (Real Decision-Making Power)

**What decisions do Engineering Managers fully own today?**

[David B.] We have full autonomy over team structure and responsibility assignment. We own our team's processes and define the ways of working that best suit our goals.

**Can you share an example where an EM’s decision directly shaped the product or team direction?**

[David B.] I implemented a weekly "focus day" free of recurring meetings to empower deep work for ICs. By structurally separating operational solutioning from feature development, we minimized context switching and successfully scaled to managing four simultaneous feature initiatives.

**Where does leadership intentionally step back to allow EMs to lead?**

[David B.] We are fully empowered to drive performance cycles, including promotions, merit increases, and bonus calculations. While senior leadership sets the vision and strategy, we have complete control over execution and how we achieve those goals.

**How do we handle situations when an EM’s decision doesn’t deliver the expected outcome?**

[David B.] We operate in a genuine no-blame culture. When a decision of mine inadvertently created extra work for senior leadership, the response was constructive coaching rather than criticism, focusing on how to navigate similar situations better in the future.

## 3. Support from Leadership

**How do senior leaders support Engineering Managers in their day-to-day work?**

[David B.] Senior leadership is incredibly approachable and available. I can DM anyone in leadership and consistently receive immediate, helpful responses, fostering a truly collaborative environment.

**What does “support” look like in practice during high-pressure situations?**

[David B.] Support means helping me step back to see the big picture. Whether I need deep domain knowledge to understand the "why" or guidance on justifying budget for staffing, my leaders are there to ensure I have the resources and context to succeed.

**How accessible is leadership when EMs need guidance or escalation?**

[David B.] Leadership is exceptionally accessible. As mentioned, the open-door policy is real here—guidance and escalation paths are clear, and responses are prompt and supportive.

**Can you recall a moment where leadership actively backed an EM’s decision?**

[David B.] Leadership actively backs our decisions, especially during critical moments like performance calibrations. I've personally experienced strong support when securing retention packages for key talent, demonstrating their trust in my judgment.
